Focus area 2

Innovation Project

A biodiversity problem, supported by evidence, followed by a solution the team designs, shares, tests, and improves.

Official 2026 prompt

What the team must solve

“Identify a problem that puts biodiversity at risk and design an innovative solution that can help.”

The team chooses the specific problem. The solution can be new or an improvement to something that already exists, but the students must explain what they learned, what they made, who reviewed it, and what changed.

Engineering NotebookJudging rubric

Five parts the judges score

1

Identify

Define one clear biodiversity-risk problem. Research it with a variety of reliable sources and examine existing solutions.

2

Design

Make a project plan, compare ideas, and show that every student contributed to development.

3

Create

Develop an original idea or meaningful improvement. Make a prototype model or detailed drawing that explains it.

4

Iterate

Share the idea with multiple relevant people or groups, record feedback, and show specific improvements based on it.

5

Communicate

Give a clear five-minute live presentation about the solution, its potential impact, and the team’s progress.

Project sparks

Useful starting directions—not assigned topics

Aerial technology

How could drones or remote sensing help people study hard-to-reach habitat without causing damage?

Local ecosystem tools

How could a tool help native plants, wildlife, or the people responsible for a nearby habitat?

Mapping and restoration

How could change-over-time data guide restoration of native plants and animals?

Wildlife monitoring

How could camera traps, tracking, or another observation method collect useful data with less disturbance?

The Engineering Notebook includes these prompts as examples. The team may select a different biodiversity problem.

What to show in five minutes

Problem and research

What is at risk, why the team chose it, the strongest evidence, and what existing solutions miss.

Solution and prototype

How the idea works, what is original or improved, and a model or detailed drawing judges can understand.

Feedback and change

Who reviewed it, what they said, what the team changed, and the potential impact and limitations.
