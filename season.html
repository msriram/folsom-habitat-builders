2026–27 roadmap

From kickoff
to tournament.

A high-level season plan that gives every meeting a purpose while leaving room for discoveries after the official game release.

Meeting rhythm

Consistent, focused, child-owned

Start with two 90-minute meetings each week. Add an optional weekend test session closer to tournament season.

Typical 90 minutes: 10-minute stand-up · 35 minutes robot · 35 minutes project · 10-minute demo and documentation.

Each meetup should end with

  • One visible result
  • One recorded test or decision
  • One problem to solve next
  • A clear owner for the next action
Season phases

High-level goals by phase

1

Kickoff and discovery · August

Understand all rules, build mission models, try robot fundamentals, list biodiversity problems, and establish team routines.

2

Foundations · Late August–September

Freeze a sturdy base robot, solve easy missions, select the project problem, interview experts, and sketch prototypes.

3

Build and test · September–October

Create short robot runs, measure repeatability, build a project prototype, collect feedback, and document iterations.

4

Integrate · October–November

Run full matches, refine attachment changes, prepare project and robot explanations, and ensure every child understands the whole solution.

5

Practice and tournament readiness

Complete mock judging, three-match simulations, backup plans, packing checklists, and a final reliability freeze.

Logistics

What families should plan for

Attendance

Regular attendance matters because robot routes and project reasoning are collaborative. Notify coaches early when a child will miss a meeting.

Materials

SPIKE Prime, field set, practice table, laptop or tablet, storage boxes, notebook, prototype supplies, and tournament transportation.

Costs

Team registration, challenge set, regional tournament fees, shirts, prototype materials, printing, snacks, and optional extra LEGO parts.
