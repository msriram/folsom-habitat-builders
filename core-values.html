Focus area 3

Core Values

Core Values are evaluated through how the team works. Students need real examples from the robot and project process—not a slogan, skit, or memorized definition.

Discovery

In practice: Learn unfamiliar biology, coding, and mechanisms; ask what the evidence does and does not show.

BIOGLOW example: Change a research question after finding a better source.

Innovation

In practice: Try multiple approaches and keep improving after failure.

BIOGLOW example: Compare two ways to manipulate a mission model or test two project prototypes.

Impact

In practice: Connect the work to a useful, realistic outcome.

BIOGLOW example: Define how a proposed solution could improve habitat conditions and how that change would be measured.

Inclusion

In practice: Make room for different ideas, abilities, and affected perspectives.

BIOGLOW example: Include local users and ecological needs when defining the project problem.

Teamwork

In practice: Share technical work, review each other’s reasoning, and solve integration problems together.

BIOGLOW example: Coding and mechanism crews test the complete run together rather than blaming one side.

Fun

In practice: Keep the work sustainable and recognize progress without turning every activity into a performance.

BIOGLOW example: Let students choose some problems and experiments they genuinely want to investigate.

Evidence worth remembering

Useful examples

  • A disagreement resolved by testing
  • A student learning a new technical skill from a teammate
  • A failed idea that changed the design
  • Feedback that changed the project
  • A decision that included an overlooked perspective

What judges may ask

  • How did you organize the work?
  • How did everyone contribute?
  • What was difficult, and what changed?
  • Where did you use Core Values outside a prepared explanation?
  • How did the team handle competing ideas?
